SMS alert — the fallback channel

For a donor with no app, or with push switched off, SMS is the only way the alert arrives. It carries exactly the same four facts as the push notification — blood group and units, urgency, approximate distance, area — and never the patient's name. Because a text message has no buttons, the donor answers with a keyword.

Accept path — the donor replies YES

CHHUB Today
CHH: O− blood needed, 2 units. Emergency. About 5 km from you, Ernakulam. Reply YES to accept, NO to decline.
09:14
YES
09:16 · Sent
CHH: Thank you. Contact Anitha Varghese on +91 90000 12345, Kochi Metro Hospital, Marine Drive. Request closes 15:14.
09:16
Alert 146 characters · 1 segment Confirmation 129 characters · 1 segment No link · no emoji · no branding line

The reveal rule is identical to the app's: the requester's name and number arrive only after the donor has said yes, and the requester gets the donor's details at the same moment. The confirmation also carries the closing time, because a donor who accepts by text has no countdown on a screen to look at.

Decline path — the donor replies NO

CHHUB Today
CHH: B+ blood needed, 1 unit. Urgent. About 12 km from you, Kalamassery. Reply YES to accept, NO to decline.
11:02
NO
11:03 · Sent
CHH: Understood, thanks for answering. You will not be messaged about this request again.
11:03
Acknowledgement 88 characters · 1 segment One reply, then the thread ends for this request — no reminder, no second ask

Declining is a legitimate answer, not a failure, and the acknowledgement says so plainly. A donor who is never thanked for a NO stops replying at all, and silence is worse than a decline — it leaves the requester waiting on someone who is not coming.

The same message at each urgency level

Emergency CHH: O− blood needed, 2 units. Emergency. About 5 km from you, Ernakulam. Reply YES to accept, NO to decline. 146
Urgent CHH: B+ blood needed, 1 unit. Urgent. About 12 km from you, Kalamassery. Reply YES to accept, NO to decline. 143
Standard CHH: A+ blood needed, 3 units. Standard. About 22 km from you, Aluva. Reply YES to accept, NO to decline. 139

Plain text has no colour and no rail, so the urgency word is doing all the work on its own — which is exactly why it must be one of the three the rest of the product uses. A donor who reads "Urgent" here and "High" in the app has to work out whether those are the same thing.

Rules for the SMS body

Always

Under 160 characters, so it sends as one segment and cannot arrive out of order · the four facts in the same order as push · at most two keywords, YES and NO, uppercase in the copy and case-insensitive in handling

Never

Patient name · requester name or number before a YES · exact address · a shortened link the recipient cannot read · emoji, branding banner or a marketing sentence

At most one link, and only to open the request in a browser — spelled out in full. This is a channel people are rightly suspicious of, and an unreadable short link from an unknown sender asking about blood is indistinguishable from a scam. The message opens with the fact and closes with the instruction; nothing sits between them.
