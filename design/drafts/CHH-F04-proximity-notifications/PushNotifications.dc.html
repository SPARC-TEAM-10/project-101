Proximity alerts — notification and feedback pieces

Push payloads carry blood group, units, urgency and approximate distance only. The patient name never leaves the requester side, on any channel. The SMS version of the same alert is on its own artboard beside this one.

Emergency gets the red rail, the filled urgency chip and a bolder title; Urgent and Standard step down to amber and neutral. The three words are always Emergency, Urgent and Standard — the same strings the request form, the cards and the SMS use. Urgency is always spelled out, never colour alone. Where the OS allows notification actions, Accept is green and Decline sits beside it: an alert a donor cannot answer honestly is one they learn to swipe away.

What every channel may and may not carry

Always

Blood group · units needed · urgency in one of the three words · approximate distance · area

Never

Patient name · requester name or number · exact address or map pin · reason for the transfusion

Distance is always phrased as approximate — "about 5 km away". A distance to one decimal place is a location disclosure dressed up as a convenience, and these land on lock screens anyone standing nearby can read.

In-app fallback when push is switched off

Push notifications are off · alerts are going out by SMS

Emergency requests still arrive in the Notification Center and as a text message, but the phone will not alert the donor when the screen is locked.

Sits at the top of the Notification Center, not as a toast: a dismissible message would hide a state the donor needs to keep seeing. SMS is the fallback channel and the only channel for a donor with no app — see the SMS artboard for the message and the reply-keyword flow.
